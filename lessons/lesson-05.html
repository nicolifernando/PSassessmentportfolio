Emotional Wellness

Have a positive attitude, high self-esteem, a strong sense of self, and the ability to recognize and share a wide range of feelings with others in a constructive way.

01Emotional wellness involves understanding and expressing feelings constructively.
02Emotions influence the way we think, behave and interact with others.
◎

What Are Emotions?

A complex psychological state involving three distinctive components:

01Subjective experience: the feeling component.
02Physiological response: the body's physical reaction.
03Behavioural response: the way an emotion is expressed through behaviour.
✦

Functions of Emotions

  • Emotions have an action orientation.
  • They add colour to our lives.
  • They are adaptive and serve a useful purpose.
  • They serve as a tipping point in dealing with dilemmas.
  • They facilitate social interactions.
Social function: emotions communicate our feelings, influence how others interact with us, facilitate social interaction, and can create, maintain or dissolve relationships.
◇

How Emotions Are Expressed

The social-expressive component is the communicative aspect of emotion. Postures, gestures, vocalizations and facial expressions make emotions public.

  • Includes verbal and non-verbal communication.
  • Helps us interpret a situation.
  • Shows how a person reacts to an event.
Mirror neurons: observing an action can trigger a simulation of that action and provide a neural basis for imitation and empathy.
♡

What Is Emotional Intelligence?

Emotional intelligence (EI) is the ability to recognize and manage emotions in ourselves and in others in order to reach desired outcomes.

EQ refers to emotional intelligence, while IQ refers to intelligence quotient.
⇄

How Does EQ Differ From IQ?

COMPARISON PRESENTED IN THE LECTURE · MHS 2005

EQ – Emotional Quotient

  • Focuses on understanding and managing emotions.
  • Can be enhanced throughout life.
  • Recognized as an important predictor of potential success.
  • Fosters understanding and management of our own emotions.
  • Promotes positive relationships.
  • Increases self-motivation and drive.

IQ – Intelligence Quotient

  • Focuses on developing cognitive abilities and is more academically oriented.
  • Traditionally considered largely established at birth.
  • Traditionally used to predict potential for success.
  • Allows development of a needed knowledge base.
  • Enables technical skills and abilities.
  • Enables conceptual thinking.
◇

Four Areas of Emotional Intelligence

The lecture discusses perceiving, using, understanding and managing emotions.

01. Perceiving Emotions

Recognizing emotions in ourselves and others through expressions and emotional signals.

02. Using Emotions

Using emotions to focus attention, reason and communicate, and to influence decision-making, creativity and problem-solving.

03. Understanding Emotions

Recognizing emotional triggers, blends of feelings, emotional changes over time and using a rich emotional vocabulary.

04. Managing Emotions

Staying open to feelings, blending emotions with thinking and reflectively monitoring emotions.

History: Peter Salovey and John Mayer developed the four-branch ability model. The lecture also mentions the Mayer-Salovey-Caruso Emotional Intelligence Test (MSCEIT).
☆

Goleman's Five Competencies

FIVE INTER-RELATED DOMAINS

Self-awareness Knowing your moods, emotions and drives, and their effect on others.
Self-regulation Controlling or redirecting disruptive impulses and thinking before acting.
Motivation Pursuing goals with energy and persistence beyond money or status.
Empathy Sensing what others feel and understanding their emotional make-up.
Social skills Handling relationships, reading social situations, finding common ground and building rapport.
▤

Emotional Intelligence at Work

  • Complex jobs require higher levels of emotional intelligence.
  • Careers of people with low EI may plateau early.
  • Technical competencies may help you get a job, while high EI can support promotion.
  • Customer satisfaction is an important issue in today's workplace.
Key idea: Technical knowledge and emotional skills both contribute to professional success.
♡

Emotions and Health

  • The lecture associates negative emotional states with unhealthy physical states.
  • Positive emotional states are associated with healthier states.
  • It highlights links with the cardiovascular and immune systems.
  • S-IgA (secretory immunoglobulin A) is described as a first line of defence in the immune system.
Emotion and environment: positive emotional states can signal safety, while negative states can act as an alert that something needs attention.
◎

Managing Emotions

01Stay open to feelings.
02Blend emotions with thinking.
03Reflectively monitor emotions.
Cognitive re-appraisal: reinterpret a situation or change thought patterns to change the emotional reaction.
Emotional contagion: emotions can spread between people. The lecture encourages making emotional contagion work in our favour.
