What are Professional Skills?

Professional skills, also known as soft skills or employability skills, are the non-technical interpersonal abilities needed for success in the workplace. They complement technical knowledge and can be transferred across different roles and industries.

Professional skills help turn knowledge into effective workplace performance.

Develop these skills through continuous learning, practice, feedback and embracing new challenges.

◎

Importance of Professional Skills

01 Enhances employability and the ability to secure a job
02 Supports success in a productive workplace
03 Encourages effective teamwork and collaboration
04 Improves communication and problem-solving
05 Creates opportunities for career advancement
♧

Classifying Workplace Skills

Workplace skills can be grouped into three main categories:

Technical Skills

Specific expertise and knowledge required for a particular job or industry.

Examples:

Programming, data analysis and using industry-specific software.

Soft Skills

Interpersonal and communication skills that improve work performance and collaboration.

Examples:

Communication, teamwork, leadership and time management.

▥

Transferable Skills

Versatile abilities that can be applied across different roles and industries:

  • Problem-solving
  • Adaptability
  • Critical thinking
  • Communication
  • Teamwork
  • Time management
  • Leadership
  • Flexibility across roles
▥

Values, Beliefs, Attitudes & Character

These four elements influence our decisions, behaviour and professional identity.

♧
Values – What is important to you Core principles that guide behaviour and decision-making, such as honesty and respect.
▣
Beliefs – What you think is true Personal convictions that shape how you see yourself and the world.
♙
Attitudes – How you respond Positive or negative ways of responding to people, situations and ideas.
◇
Character – The qualities you demonstrate Moral and ethical qualities shown through consistent actions and behaviour.
⚙

Johari Window

A model of self-awareness and interpersonal relationships developed by Joseph Luft and Harrington Ingham in 1955.

01 Open Area – Known to yourself and others
02 Blind Area – Known to others, unknown to yourself
03 Hidden Area – Known to yourself, unknown to others
04 Unknown Area – Not yet known to yourself or others
05 Feedback and open communication can increase self-awareness
